One of those was Norway's National Centre for Epilepsy, where - in what the page
calls a pioneering application - a NORD-2B processed **live EEG signals from
patients to automatically detect and quantify certain types of epileptic
seizure**.

On dating, the page is careful: "Possibly 1970, or at least development finishing
in 1970", supported by two outside quotations - that in 1970 ND teamed with
Siemens on a packet-switching network based on "Norsk Data's new Nord 2B
machines" for the Norwegian Air Force Supply Service, and that the **NORD-2B
Hardware Manual is dated December 1970**.

memory, talking to each other over a data channel and sharing a common I/O system.

Survivor: **NORD-2B serial 05** in the Telemuseum collection, marked 8019.

**Two manuals exist and are linked from the page** - NORD-2B Hardware Manual

as PDF and OCR'd PDF, from the Tingo collection. Neither is in this repo.

Little is known about the NORD-2B. It appears to have specs similar to the NORD-1, with 4kw - 16kw core memory. It was a simplified and less costly version of the NORD-1. A substantial number of NORD-2B computers were installed at CERN in Geneve, and also at various Norwegian sites. One of these was Norway's National Centre for Epilepsy (named "Statens Senter for Epilepsi" at the time). In a pioneering application the NORD-2B was used to process live electroencephalography (EEG) signals from patients to automatically detect and quantify certain types of epileptic seizures.

Front view of the NORD-2B in the Telemuseum collection.

Year of introduction: Possibly 1970, or at least development finishing in 1970. From an article:
"In 1970, ND teamed with Siemens and obtained a contract to build a packet-switching network (based on Norsk Data's new Nord 2B machines) for the Norwegian Air Force Supply Service."
Also, the NORD-2B Hardware Manual is dated December 1970.

From "Shipping World and Shipbuilder", 1972: 
"Two NORD 2B computers (each with a 16k memory) communicate with each other through a data channel (ACM) and are connected to the process through a common I/O system. This makes it possible to run PAC functions on both computers."
